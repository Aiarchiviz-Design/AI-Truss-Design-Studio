CadTech Truss Joint Fix V9.9  (flush connections between perpendicular trusses)
==============================================================================
Problem: some trusses ran THROUGH perpendicular trusses (e.g. front jacks through a side jack, stepdown through front
jacks, hip carrier through a side jack) instead of stopping at the side face.
Causes (app/roof.js):
 1. The receiver search only looked at Girders and Hip carriers, so a truss ending on a side jack / front jack /
    stepdown / common got NO connection and was never cut.
 2. The side-jack "side-flush" rule kept the lowest end; when that lowest end was the end touching the receiver it
    could not tell which side to keep.
 3. Only one connection per truss was allowed, so a truss ending on a receiver at BOTH ends was cut at one end only.
Fix:
 - New generic "end-flush" connection: any truss end that lands on the body of a non-parallel truss is cut flush to
   that truss's near face. Corner contacts (both ends meet, e.g. hips/valleys) are left alone.
 - Side-flush keeps the end FARTHEST from the receiver when the lowest end is the touching one.
 - Up to two connections per truss (one per end), never two at the same point.
Tests: node app/test-end-flush-v99.mjs (new). Example roof TRUSSES.dxf: trusses passing through a perpendicular truss 20 -> 9
(remaining: corner/overhang crossings between full trusses and the diagonal hip-carrier case, where a diagonal cut leaves
up to ~15 mm of corner overlap).
Cache-busting bumped (connection-geometry v13, roof v5). Hard-refresh (Ctrl+F5).

V9.9.1 - trusses still not reaching their perpendicular trusses
 1. A truss that CARRIES other trusses was being cut by them. Example: side jack SJ17 carries front jacks FJ12-FJ16, but
    the side-flush rule also trimmed SJ17 at FJ12/FJ13, so it stopped about 0.6 m short of common truss C07. The rule now
    skips a receiver whose own end lands on this truss (that truss is the one that gets cut, not this one).
 2. A jack whose raised end stops short of a perpendicular truss (SJ14 ended 423 mm before front jack FJ17) is extended
    along its own slope to that truss, up to 0.6 m and only if the height meets within 120 mm, then connected flush.
    The extension is recorded as t.extendedEnd (mm).
Tests: test-end-flush-v99.mjs now also covers SJ17 and SJ14. Cache-busting: roof.js v6.
Still open: corner/overhang crossings between full trusses (C07/C08) and the diagonal hip-carrier corner overlap (~15 mm).

V9.9.2 - full-width end stud where a truss butts a perpendicular truss
 Problem: the connections were right, but the vertical stud at the cut end was a thin sliver. The end node lies on the
 receiver's centre line, so cutting at the receiver's near face removed most of the stud (about 25 mm of 89 mm left).
 Fix (app/connection-geometry.js, endStudAdjust): the end stud is moved inward until its OUTSIDE face is flush with the
 receiver face and it keeps its full width; its top follows the top-chord underside. The diagonal web that lands on the
 same node is trimmed back to the stud's inner face so they do not overlap. Only true studs (both nodes on the same
 X) are moved; steep diagonals are not treated as studs. Top/bottom chords still run to the receiver face.
 Test: test-end-flush-v99.mjs checks every end stud at a flush cut is full width (67 in the example roof; fails on V9.9.1).
 Note: the manufacturing cut list (memberCutProfile) is unchanged - this affects the 3D solids.
 Cache-busting: connection-geometry v14.

V9.9.3 - no overhang where two eaves meet
 Problem: at re-entrant eave corners (and where an eave end meets a perpendicular truss) the overhang tails of the two
 eaves crossed each other or ran through the neighbouring truss.
 Fix: roof.js records, for every eave end, what its tail would hit along the end direction in plan (t.eaveHits: another
 truss body, or another truss's tail). advanced.js templateMembers() skips the overhang at that end when the hit lies within
 the tail length (the other truss's tail length for tail-to-tail hits; hip carriers use their 45-degree plan factor).
 Both tails of a crossing pair are removed. Ordinary eaves keep their overhang.
 Example roof TRUSSES.dxf: 15 of 101 overhangs removed (all at eave intersections), 0 trusses run through a perpendicular
 truss (was 9). Simple gable/hip roof (nw-6th-st.obj): none removed.
 Test: node app/test-eave-clash-v99.mjs. Overhang is recomputed when templates are rebuilt / roof regenerated.
 Cache-busting: roof v7, advanced v4.

V9.9.4 - wood take-off
 The roof app had no usable wood take-off: the PDF only listed pieces per role and every chord appeared as many short
 panel-length pieces. New (app/takeoff.js):
  * "Wood take-off" button in the header (next to Export model): table by section with pieces, cut lin ft, nominal board
    feet, boards to order by stock length (8-24 ft), spliced pieces and yield, plus download links.
  * Export dialog now also contains wood-takeoff-summary.csv, wood-cutlist.csv (every board: truss, mark, role, section,
    plies, qty, cut length ft-in and mm, stock length, BF, note) and wood-takeoff-by-truss.csv.
  * PDF report: the old minimal table is replaced by a full WOOD TAKE-OFF page (totals, by section, stock boards, by role,
    by truss).
 How it counts:
  * Collinear top-chord / overhang segments (and bottom-chord segments) that share nodes and the same section are merged
    into ONE continuous board; the overhang is part of its rafter. Webs are single boards.
  * Length = long-point cut length of the actual cut solid (so the trimmed web / stud / flush-cut ends are included).
  * Plies multiply the quantity (2-ply girders = 2 boards). Section comes from the analysis (sectionName); trusses not yet
    analyzed use the template 2x4 and the panel says so.
  * Ordering: first-fit-decreasing into 8,10,12,14,16,18,20,24 ft stock with 1/8 in kerf; a chord longer than 24 ft is a
    spliced piece (24 ft board + remainder). Board feet are nominal (2x4 = 0.667 BF/ft). Add your own waste allowance.
 Example roof TRUSSES.dxf (template 2x4): 1476 members -> 1010 boards, 4430 lin ft, 2953 BF.
 Test: node app/test-takeoff-v99.mjs. Cache-busting: connection-geometry v15, report v4, takeoff v1.

V9.9.5 - truss drawings in the Design Report in shop-drawing style (app/truss-drawing.js)
 Reference: attic/gable truss shop drawings (lumber with plates, dimension strings, call-outs, pitch marker, cut marks).
 Each truss page now has:
  * Lumber drawn from the SAME cut outlines as the 3D model (flush heel/ridge cuts, seated webs, full-width end studs).
  * Connector plates drawn at every real joint (not at continuous chord points) - schematic location only, size/orientation
    by plate design.
  * Dimension method: bottom-chord panel points; overhang | bearing span | overhang; OVERALL width; overall height and end
    heights; arrowheads and extension lines; feet-inches to 1/16 in (or metric when the report is exported metric).
  * Call-outs in a row above the truss (TOP CHORD / WEBS / BOTTOM CHORD with section, plies and the project lumber grade)
    with vertical leaders; pitch triangle (rise/12); title "G01 - GIRDER TRUSS - span - pitch" underlined like the references.
  * Member marks (TC1, BC1, W1...) on the drawing that tie to a board-level member schedule below it: mark, role, section,
    plies, cut length (long point, chord segments merged, overhang included), cut angles A/B from square, force, ratio and
    governing case (worst segment of a merged chord). Marks, lengths and board counts are identical to the wood take-off.
  * The compact two-column design information table leaves room for the drawing; flat trusses get a shorter drawing area.
 Test: node app/test-truss-drawing-v99.mjs (82 trusses drawn; schedule = take-off boards).
 Cache-busting: connection-geometry v16, takeoff v2, truss-drawing v2, report v5.

V9.9.6 - connector plates smaller than the wood (truss-drawing.js)
 Plates were sized from 1.55 x the member depth, so they stood proud of the lumber. They are now sized from the CHORD depth:
 78% of the chord depth across the chord and about 1.45 x the chord depth along it, so lumber shows around every plate.
 Orientation unchanged (along the bottom chord at bottom-chord joints, along the top chord elsewhere). Still schematic only.
 Cache-busting: truss-drawing v3.

V9.9.8 - AUTO-DESIGN: every truss ends PASS or FAIL (no more "DESIGN REQUIRED" / "REVIEW")
 Why you saw those statuses: the old checks needed numbers the app did not have (hold-down and plate capacity default to 0, so
 every truss said DESIGN REQUIRED), flagged long compression members for REVIEW, flagged girders "TRANSFER LOADS - reanalysis
 required", and stopped at 3 plies / 89 mm bearing.
 "Analyze" now designs automatically (app/autodesign.js, engineering.js, advanced.js, engine.js):
  1 Member sizes 2x4..2x12 per chord/web group, plies 1..5 (was 3), web pattern and interior bearings (unless "End bearings only").
  2 Jack / hip-carrier reactions are applied to the girder or carrier that carries them as NODAL LOADS (per load case, at the
    connection point, shared between the two top-chord nodes) and the girder is re-designed - repeated until the loads stop
    changing (chains such as jack -> hip carrier -> girder converge in a few rounds). Equilibrium verified.
  3 Bearing: bearing length 89 -> 140 mm (5.5 in) if needed; if still short, plies are added (longer bearings are not realistic).
  4 Uplift: hold-down requirement sized to the demand. If you enter a hold-down capacity it is used (up to 2 per bearing).
  5 Joints: required capacity per joint stated from the largest member force. If you enter a plate capacity it is checked.
  6 Long compression members get lateral-restraint points (<= brace trigger unbraced length) instead of "REVIEW".
 Result per truss and for the roof: PASS or FAIL with the reason. FAIL happens when no combination up to 2x12 / 5-ply works
 (e.g. extreme load, "End bearings only"), when an entered hold-down / plate capacity is too small, or a bearing cannot be met.
 Shown in: Analyze results table (Result column, roof result line, failure reasons, auto-design changes), PDF summary page, each
 truss page (DESIGN CHECK table, DESIGN RESULT line, automatic design changes, braced members, transfer table).
 Inputs renamed: Hold-down capacity (0 = size to demand), Joint/plate capacity (0 = state requirement).
 IMPORTANT - preliminary screening: with capacity 0 the hold-down and plate checks PASS by stating the requirement (kN) that the
 connector must develop; the product / plate design and bracing details still need engineering verification.
 Tests: node app/test-autodesign-v99.mjs. Cache-busting: advanced v5, engineering v2, engine v4, autodesign v1, report v7.
